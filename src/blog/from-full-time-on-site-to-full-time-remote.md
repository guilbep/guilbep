# From full-time on-site to full-time remote

## TL;DR

The 2026 question isn't "remote vs office" — it's whether your company is async-native or just running the office over video.

## Why it matters

_(Draft prompt: most "I switched to remote" posts get derailed by the 2025–2026 RTO mandate news. Reframe the personal transition as a shift in operating model, not a change of address. That sidesteps the tired debate and is more honest about what changed.)_

## Current state

- **2025–2026 RTO wave is real but uneven**: Amazon went 5-day; most of Big Tech (Google, Meta, Salesforce, Dell, Apple, IBM) settled on 3-day hybrid. Gartner reports ~75% of HR leaders see internal tension, **13% turnover bump at strict-RTO firms**.
- **Buffer 2025**: 98% want remote at least part-time; stress trending up; working parents disproportionately exposed — **flexibility, not location, is the durable demand**.
- **Hybrid converging on the "anchor-day" pattern** (Tue/Wed/Thu in office) — often the worst of both worlds: commute cost without spontaneity payoff, async discipline without async benefits.
- **The real cultural fault line is async-first vs sync-first**, not remote vs office. Most "remote-friendly" employers just moved the meeting room to Zoom; remote-native (GitLab, Doist, Basecamp) rebuilt around written artefacts and time-zone independence.
- **Cost-of-living arbitrage tightened**: location-based pay bands, state/country tax-nexus rules, EU posted-worker / permanent-establishment risk. Parallel signal: the 6-country **4-day-week trial** (Nature, 2025) suggests the next frontier is hours, not location.

## Angle worth leaning into

**"Remote-friendly is not remote-native."** This frames your transition as a shift in operating model (written-first, asynchronous, outcome-measured) rather than a change of address — both more honest and more defensible against the 2025–2026 RTO backlash narrative.
